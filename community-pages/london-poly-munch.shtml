What it's like

Newcomer friendly and as inclusive as the organisers can make it: all backgrounds, genders, orientations and relationship shapes are welcome, from singles to polycules to curious monogamous friends. Many regulars — and all the organisers — are neurodivergent, and disabled guests are very welcome.

Flirting is welcome too: bringing people together is a big part of why the munch exists. The group is strict about consent — talking is great, but always ask first, and if there's any doubt, don't. In over ten years, organisers have needed to step in only a handful of times; people are almost always charming and well behaved.

Brief history

The munch started around 2014–15 as a small local munch in Kilburn. The organisers had been searching for a "London Poly Munch" and kept finding the one in London, Canada, so they started their own. Members asked for weekend socials, and the group began heading to parties together afterwards. Too many venues weren't poly friendly — a "couples room" that turned away one of their triads, dungeons that put the bar before the play — so the organisers started Panacea: parties that welcome groups of any size and put play ahead of drinking.

How to join

Listed on the Panacea website and on the FetLife group. RSVPs on FetLife are optional, and first names are fine. There's also a monthly email update — sign up on the munch page. You can also contact the organisers directly by email.

Guidelines & privacy

  • Be friendly and discreet — it's a public bar.
  • Consent and respect apply to conversation too; see the full FAQ.
  • No photos of other guests, and please don't share who you saw there.

Accessibility

The munch is on the first floor of the West 12 shopping centre, reached by lift. Food and soft drinks are served, so it isn't alcohol-centred. At Panacea's parties, the venue's ground floor is step-free from the street; the upper floor is stairs only, though the crew are happy to help anyone who needs it.
