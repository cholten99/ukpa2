What it's like

Polystation is queer in its origins and its values, and open to everyone. It's one of the most diverse groups you'll find, across gender, age, faith, ethnicity, race and sexuality. The organisers believe we learn most from people whose experiences are different from our own, so everybody is welcome as long as they come with respect and follow the group's guidelines.

Flirting isn't what the group is for, but it's part of how people connect. As long as you respect the space and everyone in it, you're welcome to just be human.

What a session looks like

  1. A check-in and a short grounding moment to arrive and connect.
  2. A round of introductions and a question on the session's topic.
  3. Breakout groups to discuss it and get to know each other.
  4. Back together as a whole group to share what came up.
  5. Often a second breakout, then closing thoughts and takeaways from everyone.

It's an engaging format, and people learn a lot from each other.

Brief history

Polystation began in 2024 as a monthly London meet-up, founded by Angel Perez and Sion Eryl as a place to connect with peers and learn from each other. It has grown into a community of well over a hundred people. The 40-person monthly meet-up regularly sells out, and an online community platform is in development. Polystation also runs sessions at festivals, longer workshops and one-to-one coaching.

How to join

All meet-up details and tickets are on the OutSavvy listing. Meet-ups often sell out, so it's worth booking early.

There's a WhatsApp group for members, but it isn't public: the organisers would rather you come along to a meet-up and become part of the community first. The link is shared there, and members can pass it on to people they know.

Guidelines & support

Polystation has ten community guidelines covering respect, curiosity, thought experimentation, non-judgement, fluidity and growth, and inclusivity. It's a space for discussion and learning, not therapy.

If anything feels off at an event, talk to a facilitator or email hello@polystation.org.

Accessibility

There's step-free access to the first floor of The Common Press, and OutSavvy offers a free ticket for an assistant. Get in touch with the organisers if you have specific access needs.
